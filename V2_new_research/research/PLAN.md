## 1. Question and constraints

Can an image-only inference system rank 12 expert-defined knee MRI findings reliably within the [Kaggle code-submission rules](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/overview/code-requirements)? The [official metric](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/overview/evaluation) is mean ROC-AUC across the 12 findings. Reports exist for all 4,407 training studies but not for test input; only 58 training studies have all 12 expert labels (verified in `data_profile.json`). Report NLP can create **training supervision**, not a test-time feature. The host's [finding definitions](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/733343) and [label clarification](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/733826) govern the target, which can differ from report language.

The public benchmark was 0.964 when queried with the authenticated Kaggle CLI on October 9. It is a moving public score and does not validate any idea in this plan. Public implementations below are **prior art and hypotheses**, with author-reported metrics, not our results.

The [competition overview](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/overview) lists an October 15 entry deadline and October 22 final submission deadline. An authenticated Kaggle CLI query on October 9 reports `userHasEntered=True` for this account and confirms **October 22, 2026, 23:59 UTC** as the competition deadline. The scored notebook must work offline within nine hours. We will verify current code rules and runtime before submission.

## 3. Measurement contract before experiments

1. Use only the official competition training cohort. Verify study/series IDs, duplicate images, DICOM geometry, report families, site/scanner proxies and available patient identifiers. Freeze a grouped study split; document residual patient-linkage uncertainty when patient IDs are unavailable.
2. Hold the 58 expert-labeled images **and all detected duplicate/patient-linked groups** out of image-model fitting. Therefore, the usable weak-supervision cohort may be smaller than 4,349 studies. Group near-duplicate/identical reports and detectable image duplicates into the same development fold. Quarantine the one nonexpert study linked to an expert study by the initial perceptual-image screen until adjudication; never consult the expert labels during this audit. Confirm or reject all cross-fold perceptual candidates with higher-resolution, multislice and acquisition-aware evidence plus unrelated matched controls before freezing the split. Similarity hashes alone do not establish identity. Union confirmed links; group or exclude unresolved cross-fold links and recheck transitive connections to expert exclusions.
3. For the revised **exploratory** M00/M01 path, freeze the [public report-label v1 source, exact hard threshold and mask rule, grouped split, and weak-validation metric](M00_THREE_EPOCH_CONTRACT.md) before training. The 885-study development validation partition has at least 20 public-proxy positives and 20 public-proxy negatives for each of twelve targets. The user's waiver allows the smaller AI packet to remain a secondary diagnostic even though it supports only two targets in both A and B. This replaces the earlier plan for a clinician-reviewed, independent 12-target binary reference; **do not describe the proxy as independent clinical evaluation or expert image-label performance**. Report every target's support/AUC, masked BCE, and paired component-level bootstrap intervals where meaningful. Resolve tied macro AUC by lower masked validation BCE, then earlier epoch. Preserve saved per-study predictions and source hashes.
4. Freeze the report-labeler, candidate selection rule, architecture and inference before opening the 58 expert labels for a predeclared final check. If those 58 subsequently influence choices, relabel them a **development set**; no longer describe later checks as untouched validation. The sample is too small for a reliable claim from tiny AUC differences.
5. The prepared 60/120 language-stratified packets were annotated by two AI passes with adjudication because human clinical review is unavailable. Their [audit](AI_REVIEW_V1_AUDIT.md) measures report-assertion proxy quality and limitations, not MRI truth. Keep the held-out B packet out of future rule or prompt revisions; its near-perfect agreement with a public report labeler does not validate image performance. Use the same split, fixed evaluation labels, preprocessing, seeds, checkpoint rule, GPU budget and output schema for every M00/M01 comparison. Record code/data hashes, learning curves, prediction files, per-target AUC, training/inference time, GPU memory, failed-study counts and notebook version. Repeat finalists across seeds only if measured resources permit.
   The training loader must exclude studies with zero observed target cells from gradient batches, log their counts by language, and never fabricate a negative label; a batch that becomes all-unobserved is skipped and counted rather than sent to the masked loss.
6. Treat public leaderboard feedback as a secondary, sparse check. A score from a public notebook or GitHub README is not a direct comparison unless data, checkpoint and submission protocol match.

## 5. Experiment backlog

Each row changes one stated factor against the frozen reference for that phase. All gains are hypotheses. First run fast correctness/throughput probes, then limited training, then full runs only for finalists. The detailed machine-readable [registry](../experiments/registry.csv) tracks status.

### A. Dataset and DICOM pilot — required before training

| ID | One change / comparison | Main question and measurements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A01 | DICOM sort by filename versus `ImagePositionPatient` projected onto the orientation normal | How often does order differ? Inspect adjacency, duplicated positions and spacing gaps. Geometry is the proposed correct policy; this is primarily a correctness test, not a leaderboard ablation. |
| A02 | Native resize versus physical-size center crop versus affine-aware in-plane resampling | Are joint dimensions consistent without losing peripheral pathology? Record pixel spacing, physical field of view, anatomy retention and pilot runtime. The public 140 mm crop is a candidate, not an adopted constant. |
| A03 | Per-series percentile scaling versus robust nonzero z-score; per-slice scaling as a diagnostic | Does normalization preserve adjacent-slice contrast? Measure saturation and train/inference pixel parity. MRI has no universal CT-like window. |
| A04 | One, three and five physical-neighbor slices per 2D window | Does local through-plane context improve focal targets? Record actual millimeter gaps and do not cross series boundaries. |
| A05 | One best series versus three planes versus five semantic roles | Which slots improve 12-target ranking per unit runtime? Use explicit missing-role masks and report role coverage. |
| A06 | Native orientation versus validated laterality-canonical orientation | Does medial/lateral consistency improve? Keep an unresolved flag; audit tag/geometry agreement and per-target errors. Do not horizontally flip without preserving label semantics. |
| A07 | On-demand DICOM decode versus deterministic predecoded memory map | Does caching buy enough training throughput to justify build time/disk? Check exact pixel equality, hashes, failures, and notebook data limits. |

### B. Report NLP and weak supervision — reports never enter inference

| ID | One change / comparison | Main question and measurements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N01 | Basic target lexicon with four states: positive, negative, uncertain, not addressed | Coverage, positive rate and disagreements with blinded manual report review. Do not convert silence automatically to negative. |
| N02 | Whole-report extraction versus Findings/Impression-aware parsing | Does section context reduce irrelevant prior/history mentions? Current profile shows both named sections appear in only a subset, so include a fallback. |
| N03 | English-only assertion rules versus language-aware negation, uncertainty, laterality and severity rules | Audit error by manually identified language and target. Follow the host's severity thresholds, not keyword presence alone. |
| N04 | Frozen multilingual encoder/tokenizer versus domain-adapted text encoder | Compare subword fragmentation, 95th-percentile sequence length, truncation rate and label-ranking quality. Test XLM-R as a candidate; an English clinical model is only a language-specific diagnostic. A custom tokenizer is considered only if measured fragmentation warrants it. |
| N05 | Full report versus section-prioritized truncation versus sliding token windows | Do long reports lose key findings? `report_profile.json` shows up to 690 whitespace words; actual subword lengths must be measured with each tokenizer. |
| N06 | Severity-aware, fixed-schema local language-model reader versus rule reader | Does it improve 12-target soft labels, uncertainty handling and evidence consistency? Audit access terms and compute; never send reports to a hosted service without a rule/data review. |
| N07 | One fixed prompt revision adding exact host definitions | Measure only this change. Avoid iterative prompt selection on the 58 expert cases. |
| N08 | Simple blend of genuinely different label sources versus source-aware weak-label model | Measure source disagreement and correlation; a more complex label model earns its place only if it improves a frozen check. |
| N09 | Hard versus defensible soft targets on **the same observed cells and fixed weights/masks** | **Second prioritized comparison if soft values have audited meaning:** does uncertainty-aware supervision help image ranking? Confidence weighting is a later separate change. If soft values are arbitrary, prioritize M02 coverage instead. |
| N10 | Image pseudo-label self-training with a prespecified high-confidence threshold | Only after baseline validation; exclude held-out studies, log added-study counts and compare against unchanged training budget. |

### C. Image architecture and learning-rate study

| ID | One change / comparison | Main question and measurements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M00 | Random-init ResNet18, three adjacent slices, three plane roles, 12 training/48 inference windows, 12 outputs, shared **max** pooling | Establish a full-study, runtime-safe baseline under the fixed weak-label reference. Record per-target rankings and where sparse lesions are missed. |
| M01 | M00 shared pooling versus 12 finding-specific attention queries | **First prioritized full comparison:** does task-specific evidence selection help focal and diffuse findings? [Attention MIL](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/ilse18a.html) is prior art, not claimed novelty. |
| M02 | 8 versus 16 versus 32 training windows, fixed full inference coverage | Is sparse-lesion coverage worth extra training cost? Match train/inference window definitions and measure variance across seeds. |
| M03 | Plane/sequence embeddings plus masks versus simple concatenated window set | Does explicit role information help when series vary? Keep encoder and window count fixed. |
| M04 | Physical-position embeddings versus none | Can position help compartment-specific targets? Check uncertainty in laterality and measured DICOM coordinates. |
| M05 | One global crop versus global plus a high-resolution local crop | Does lesion detail improve without losing whole-knee context? Require correct anatomy coverage and a runtime cap. |
| M06 | Compact encoder versus eligible DINOv2-S versus CoAtNet | Only after input and pooling are fixed. Compare full-run AUC and cost; do not select a larger model from a short proxy alone. The [public CoAtNet repository](https://github.com/mathischmp/challenge_rsna) reports 0.920 and several negative trials, all author-reported. |
| M07 | Uniform LR versus discriminative backbone/head LR | Start with AdamW; compare e.g. one LR to lower pretrained-backbone LR plus higher new-head LR, holding effective batch, schedule and epochs fixed. |
| M08 | Cosine with warmup versus OneCycle schedule | Compare paired full learning curves and checkpoint stability, not only a quick proxy. |
| M09 | Constant BCE versus clipped per-target positive weight versus masked soft-label BCE | Does imbalance handling help ranking or amplify weak-label errors? Keep prediction selection on AUC, inspect minority targets. |
| M10 | Mild intensity jitter versus none; small spatial motion in a separate trial | Test scanner robustness without anatomically implausible flips/deformation. Use [MONAI transforms](https://monai.readthedocs.io/en/stable/transforms.html) where their behavior fits the audited geometry. |
| M11 | Single model versus a second seed; blend only if errors differ | Estimate training noise and error correlation before spending a full extra run. Never tune blend weights on the same tiny expert check. |

## 6. Priorities under the calendar

**Proposed compute cap:** CPU audits first; at most two image-model pilots of one GPU-hour each, then one full baseline run and one prespecified full comparison, each capped at eight GPU-hours; reserve one nine-hour Kaggle notebook window for the final scored inference. These are upper bounds, **not measured availability**. This budget funds M00 and, conditionally, M01; N09 is a later backlog item. Stop early if throughput makes the limit implausible. **By October 12:** finish the correctness and runtime pilot, frozen split/label contract and offline inference smoke. **By October 15:** aim for one valid baseline submission. **By October 19:** attempt at most one prespecified full-run improvement based on pilot evidence. **By October 20:** freeze the best eligible model and leave time for Kaggle rerun/debug before October 22. This is an operating schedule, not a promise to complete every backlog item or reach a particular score.

First priority after confirmation: A01 geometry, label/split integrity, a small language-stratified manual report review, N01 basic assertion states, tokenizer **measurement** (not custom training), A07 cache parity if caching, and D04 notebook smoke; then M00. A02–A06 and all other rows remain a ranked research backlog. An experiment advances only if its inputs, paired comparison and runtime can be verified. A public reference's reported 0.920, its 140 mm crop, 64 slices or learning rates are **candidates to test**, not defaults we assume correct.
