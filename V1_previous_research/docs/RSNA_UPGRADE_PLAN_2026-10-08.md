## Competition contract

The [official evaluation page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/overview/evaluation) defines the score as the mean ROC AUC over 12 findings. The [official overview](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/overview) states that submission is through a **Notebook**, with internet disabled, a nine-hour CPU/GPU limit, and an output named `submission.csv`. The listed final submission deadline is October 22, 2026 at 11:59 PM UTC. External data and pretrained models must meet the competition's accessibility rules.

## What the public code actually demonstrates

- [nartaa's public 0.949 notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/nartaa/rsna-knee-0949-anatomical-mirror) describes a single CoAtNet checkpoint, trained at 384 pixels, using 96 slices/94 windows and plain plus anatomical-mirror inference at a native 320-pixel crop. Its author reports a 0.949 public leaderboard score and about 26 minutes submit-to-score. The notebook says training used multiple weak-label sources and OAI external data. This is an author-reported public result, not our result or a verified private score.
- [skarin's public 0.943 stack](https://www.kaggle.com/code/skarin/rsna-knee-reproducing-the-0-943-public-stack) combines 20 DINOv2 members, five attention-pooling folds, RadImageNet heads, Raptor and CoAtNet readers. It uses semantic slots, geometry/laterality handling, target-dependent fusion, checkpoint pins and output checks. Its reported scoring time is roughly seven hours.
- A [public 0.949 blend notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/goodpjw2008/rsna-knee-0-949-coatnet-stack-blend-lb-0-949) reports that mixing 60% of the single CoAtNet with 40% of the 0.943 stack also scored 0.949. Its author explicitly says the blend did not improve the stronger model. More ensemble members alone are therefore a weak improvement hypothesis.
- The local historical public-notebook audit identified semantic sequence slots, laterality normalization, denser study coverage, SSL transformer features, multiple folds and rank fusion as differences from our older ResNet18 experiments. Its earlier two ~0.94 notebooks were one related code family, not two independent replications.

## Ordered experiments

1. **Establish a legal, runnable reference.** Run the published single-model notebook with its pinned public checkpoint in an internet-off Kaggle notebook, verify the model's data/weight terms and SHA-256, test all 3 visible studies, and commit a notebook submission. Record runtime, public score, exact notebook version and checkpoint hash. Keep this labeled as a reproduced public baseline, separate from our own model.
2. **Fix evaluation before model tuning.** Audit the 58 observed labels and report-derived labels; compare teacher sources and adjudicate a small stratified sample without editing validation labels. Freeze a source split and source/hash-group receipt. Report 12-target fold-1 AUC with per-target positive/negative counts and grouped intervals; MCL and synovitis each have only two negatives on that fold, so treat their AUCs as fragile. Do not select improvements solely on the old nine-target screening fold or repeatedly tune to the public leaderboard.
3. **Finish a matched full-cohort control and candidate.** Complete the 4,407-study `p0` preprocessing and train `r00` and `r01` on the exact same cache and fold. Save `best.pt`, `run_summary.json`, `validation_predictions.csv`, cache audit and hashes. Compare selected epoch by BCE and report all 12 target metrics. This resolves whether the old `r01` advantage survives the full cohort.
4. **Run one-factor representation/coverage tests.** Pilot and measure DICOM decode, GPU memory and inference time. Then compare, one at a time, `p1` full field of view, semantic fluid/T1 slots, laterality normalization, and denser slice/window coverage. Use the same labels, folds, backbone and training budget for each matched comparison. Promote a change only after it improves the frozen evaluation with acceptable coverage and nine-hour inference headroom.
5. **Try a stronger model and evidence-based blend.** Evaluate a pretrained DINOv2 or compact CoAtNet reader on the selected preprocessing. Generate out-of-fold predictions before fitting blend weights. Blend only if the new model improves the same held-out 12-target metric and adds complementary errors; check against the public 0.949 single-model reference without treating the leaderboard as a tuning set.
6. **Package our own submission.** Refit the frozen recipe on eligible training studies, attach its checkpoint as a Kaggle model/dataset, run offline inference on test series, validate schema/UIDs/probabilities and save `submission.csv`. Submit the notebook version and record the public score and runtime. Do not claim the local 0.795 or a public notebook's 0.949 as our achieved leaderboard score.

## Immediate priority

The highest-value next run is the matched full-cohort `r00`/`r01` comparison. The fastest competition reference is the published single-model notebook. The latter gives a leaderboard yardstick; the former tells us whether our own method improved.
